## The models and their moments

Every model is one global CatBoost over the ten airports, fitted on 2025 (targets above 2 h and wrong-date rows left out of the fit). Each predicts at a fixed moment and reads only what exists at that moment:

| 3 h ahead (schedule - 3 h) | `op_hl3_r4_rwyblind.yaml` | 86 | deliverable, refitted on 2025, predicts the final set |
| H1, 15 min before the last filed off-block | `op_h1_f1_plan.yaml` | 131 | deliverable (headline): F1 since 13 Oct, `op_h1_r4_nodoy.yaml` plus the other flights' plans after the anchor (findings 10.37); three seeds refitted on 2025, predicts the final set |
| H0, at the off-block (2025 only) | `op_h0_r4_nodoy.yaml` | 118 | holdout only (2026 blanks the off-blocks): the member at the push, for comparison |

H1 has been F1 since 13 Oct (findings 10.37): the H1 deliverable until then, `op_h1_r4_nodoy.yaml` (116 columns, rule L's choice), plus the other flights' plans after the anchor (15 columns: 0 tier F, 12 O*, 3 O; Part B, *Other flights' plans after the anchor*). The H1 sensitivities Part B names (`op_h1_r4_nodoy_noplan`, `_notaf`, `_rwyblind`) were measured on `op_h1_r4_nodoy.yaml`.

**The information contract**, enforced by the leakage gates in `tests/test_operational.py`, `tests/test_operational_long.py` and, for the plans after the anchor, `tests/test_operational_future.py`:
- The flight's own takeoff and its own off-block record are never inputs. Neither are its own NM actuals (AOBT_3, ARVT_3) or EOBT_1.
- Other flights' events count only when they happened at or before the anchor.
- Other flights' plans count as known at the anchor, also where they lie after it: the schedule (O) and the last filed off-blocks (O*: a filed plan can still change after the anchor). The long models read the scheduled demand around the planned off-block, H1 the plans of the next hour (findings 10.37).
- A METAR counts from 5 min after its observation. A TAF counts only when issued at or before the anchor.
- Statistics that read targets or NM readings come from the fit rows only, month-out. Target-free tables (the arrivals' taxi-in medians) are built over the whole frame: transductive in time, with no departure target.

Rule T's first step, F1, adds only the other flights' plans after the anchor (the third rule) and has been the H1 deliverable since 13 Oct (findings 10.37). Part D's other concept members break the second rule on purpose: beyond F1 they read the other flights' realised movements after the anchor (tier F). Two of them are delivered as separate tier F files beside the H1 deliverable, never instead of it: rule T's member, `op_h1_f4_nodepthru` (`h1f`), an upper bound for perfect plans, and, at the owner's request, the full concept F5, `op_h1_f5_full` (`h1f5`, findings 10.37), whose realised departure side carries traces of the own slot, so its gain is an upper bound even for perfect plans. Their gates are in `tests/test_operational_future.py`.

**Tiers.**
- **O**, operational: an airport or NM holds the value at the anchor.
- **O***: available under an assumption the data cannot check; the reason is named in the cell. The main cases:
  - the planned departure runway is assumed equal to the one recorded;
  - hours ahead, the stand plan is assumed final;
  - other flights' recorded runway or stand stands in for the one planned;
  - at H1, the last filed off-block times are assumed known at the anchor: the flight's own 15 min ahead, the other flights' up to an hour ahead (the plans after the anchor).
- **C**: an artefact of how the challenge data were built (the NM-join level of the flight-plan columns). It is constant on the NM-joined population, where the models are read.
- **F** (Part D only): *planned in the concept, realised in the challenge data*. The other flights' realised movements after the anchor stand in for the plans of the arrival, departure and surface managers (AMAN, DMAN, A-SMGCS). An F input is not available as computed; a gain from it is an upper bound for perfect plans. An F model is read beside the deliverable, never instead of it.

**The applicability domain.** NM-joined departures, i.e. movements NM matched to a flight plan: 98.9 % of the 2025 departures and 98.5 % of the January and July 2026 ranking departures. The unjoined rest holds the stale and wrong-date block times (in 2025, 42 % of the target's squared deviation). The models still predict those rows, but nothing is claimed there.

**Evidence columns.**
- *gain %*: CatBoost's loss-change share of the column in the run named below, in % of the model.
- *block perm.*: the holdout RMSE rise in seconds when the column's whole block is shuffled, ± its std over repeats, measured on the run named below. Every column of a block shows its block's value.
  - gain, H1, 15 min before the last filed off-block: `2026-10-13T07-54-55__op_h1_f1_plan__40ac5f36__s44`
  - gain, 3 h ahead (schedule - 3 h): `2026-10-11T15-38-22__op_hl3_r4_rwyblind__c93d69e3__s43`
  - gain, 24 h ahead (schedule - 24 h): `2026-10-11T16-13-52__op_hl24_r3_rwyblind__930c7b15__s43`
  - block permutation, H1, 15 min before the last filed off-block: `2026-10-08T02-37-29__op_h1_r4__3fcdc779__s42`

**Never read by the deliverables:**

- the flight's own takeoff time (MVT_TIME_UTC_mvt): it is the outcome the taxi-out leads to; the challenge gives it, an operator does not have it before the movement.
- the flight's own recorded off-block (BLOCK_TIME_UTC_mvt): it is the target's other end; it is read only as the H0 anchor in the 2025 lab member, never as a value.
- the flight's own AOBT_3_flt and ARVT_3_flt: NM actuals of the flight itself; AOBT_3 is back-computed from the takeoff at LTFM and partly at EDDM, LEBL, LSZH and LEMD (findings 10.25).
- EOBT_1_flt: it behaves like a late-updated estimate (closer to the recorded off-block than the plan), and its update time is not in the data.
- LOBT_flt as an input of its own: it stands in for the IOBT where that is missing (the iobt data point: the H1 anchor, the planned delay, the other flights' plans), and the long models do not read it at all.
- IOBT_flt in the 3 h and 24 h models: its value hours ahead is unknown (see the data point); since findings 10.35 the long models run on the schedule.
- ADS-B positions (adsb.lol): the flight's own track is tier M. Other aircraft's positions up to the anchor need a live feed; the lab rung R6 reads them and gained nothing on the five month pairs (+0.05 s of MAE, findings 10.35), so no deliverable reads them. After the anchor the tier F concept members of Part D read them. Of the two delivered as tier F files beside the deliverable, rule T's member (`op_h1_f4_nodepthru`, `h1f`) reads only the arrivals' taxi-ins; the full concept F5 (`op_h1_f5_full`, `h1f5`) reads all of them, up to the anchor as well.
- ERA5 reanalysis and the PRU daily delay data: published after the fact (tier P).
- the NM join window, stale and wrong-date block times: artefacts of how the challenge data were built (tier C); the operational models are fitted without targets above 2 h or wrong-date rows and read on the NM-joined population.

### Departure runway

**Why it matters.** The runway fixes the end of the route: its length, its crossings and the holding point where the queue forms. Runway configurations shift the mean taxi-out by minutes.

**Literature.** Idris 2002: configuration means from about 15 to 23 min at Boston. Park & Kim 2022: operating direction is significant. Robinson 2013: runway assignment is a variance source.

**Built from:** `RUNWAY_mvt`, `STAND_mvt`.

**Caveats.** Tier O* everywhere. The data hold the runway actually used, and the models assume the planned one is known. At H1 the actual runway equals the dominant departure runway of the hour before the planned off-block on 66.3 % of departures, and lies in the set of runways in use on 93.1 %. The 3 h and 24 h models are runway-blind: hours ahead the recorded runway matches the dominant runway of the hour before the anchor on only 53 % (3 h and 24 h), and lies among the runways in use on 75 % (docs/op_runway_knowability.txt).

### Realised surface picture after the anchor: public ADS-B

**In operations, and the stand-in here.** *(not in the data) the surface manager's plan (A-SMGCS planning and routing)*: planned by the surface manager; the realised ADS-B surface events of the other aircraft stand in.

**Why it matters.** The aircraft pushing, taxiing, de-icing and holding around the flight's own taxi are the surface picture an A-SMGCS plans. Public ADS-B shows it where reception is good.

**Literature.** Wang 2020: other aircraft taxiing at the push (group B) and their recent speeds (group D).

**Caveats.** Tier F: not available as computed. In operations the value comes from a plan with its own errors, so the gain is an upper bound for perfect plans. The own movement is excluded, but traces of it remain through the resources it shares with the others (one runway slot, the next arrival at the own stand, the pier's pushback lanes, the de-icing pads, the holding queue). Rule T removes the departure-side traces by construction: its member, `op_h1_f4_nodepthru` (delivered as `h1f`), reads no realised departure-side column, and nothing from that side is credited. Three traces stay in the member: the next arrival at the own stand (unmeasured); the missingness of its ADS-B taxi-in columns, which depends on finding the own ADS-B takeoff (about 0.15 s of MAE for F4); and, where a runway is in mixed mode, the landings on the own runway (unmeasured; findings 10.36). The full concept F5, delivered at the owner's request as the second tier F file (`h1f5`, findings 10.37), reads the realised departure side as well: its gain carries traces of the own slot, which rule T could not credit, so it is an upper bound even for perfect plans. The own airframe is identified through its own ADS-B takeoff and its whole ground stay is removed; a departure whose own takeoff is not found is NaN. Coverage-gated (NaN, never zero, where reception is not established); no reception at LTFM and little at LFPG.
